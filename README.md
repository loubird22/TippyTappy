Service Information Platform
A place for the ideas I keep returning to in human service systems, and an attempt to connect, interpret and work around disconnected ones.

What is this?
A personal project exploring how human service organisations can move beyond activity reporting and better understand demand, complexity and change over time.
Most systems tell us what happened using definitions that often don't connect particularly well to the reality of service delivery. They can tell us how many clients, referrals or support periods existed, while telling us very little about complexity, intensity, cumulative risk, changing demand, or whether our responses are actually effective.

Social impact modelling is all the rage, but a lot of it needs some love in practice.

Why?
Because too much valuable information stays trapped, mishandled and then totally misreported upon to promote political whims that harm marginalised people?

Community, homelessness and social service organisations are often expected to meet increasingly complex reporting requirements while working across fragmented systems, spreadsheet extracts, duplicate data entry and inconsistent definitions.

That creates a situation where understanding becomes something only well-resourced organisations can afford.

I'd much rather see services spending their energy on things like Aboriginal Data Sovereignty, genuinely trauma-informed practice, service quality and community outcomes than wrestling with reporting architecture.

So, rather than continuing to complain about it, I figured I should try learning how to build something better.

Core Idea
Capture operational information once and derive many uses from it.

Rather than building reports directly from cases and contacts, the platform explores the idea of generating reusable service profiles that work across service areas, funding obligations and reporting frameworks.

Examples include:

Need domains
Complexity factors
Service intensity
Episode patterns
Return rates
Resolution rates
Demand trajectories
Workforce pressure indicators
Concepts Being Explored
Episode-based service models
Presenting units (individuals, households and families)
Demand fingerprinting
Complexity and acuity modelling
Workforce and resource planning
Cumulative and intergenerational risk
Information governance and auditability
Record lifecycle management and archival readiness
Multiple reporting views from a single source of truth
Flexible reporting architectures
Current Status
Very early-stage architecture and proof-of-concept work.

Right now I'm focused on data structures, service modelling and information architecture rather than user interfaces or analytics. One problem at a time.
